# The free custom skeleton (Aevon's lead offer, decided 2026-07-26)

## Why this exists
The Front Desk agent is a PRODUCT, and as a product it competes against a crowded field of
cheaper tools with years of track record behind them. Aevon has no track record, so competing
product-to-product is a losing frame.

Aevon's real advantage is not a product at all. It is that a SaaS company cannot build anything
around one specific business, and Aevon can. So the lead offer should be the thing no competitor
is able to answer:

> **Tell me the one process your team does by hand. I'll build you a working skeleton of it,
> for your business, free. If it's useful we finish it. If not, you keep it and we're done.**

An off-the-shelf competitor's only possible response is "here's our free trial of our generic
tool." They physically cannot match a bespoke build. That asymmetry is the whole point.

## What a skeleton is (and is not)
- A real, working, narrow slice of the thing: their data, their process, their words. Clickable.
- NOT a mockup, NOT slides, NOT a generic demo with their logo dropped on it.
- Scope it to ONE process and a few days of work. If it cannot be skeletoned in that time,
  it is too big for a free build — quote it instead.
- It is theirs either way. Say so. That is what makes the offer credible rather than a hook.

## Why give it away
- It replaces a discovery call that most prospects will not take, with an artifact they will open.
- It proves the only claim that matters ("built around how you actually work") by doing it,
  not describing it.
- It qualifies hard: a business that will not describe one manual process was never going to buy.
- The build cost is mostly Aidan's time, and the work is reusable across similar businesses.

## Guardrails
- ONE process, not a system. Say no to "can you also...".
- Cap it: offer it to a limited number at a time. It creates urgency honestly and lets you stop
  offering it later without changing your story.
- Do NOT wire anything into their live customer traffic for free. A skeleton runs on sample or
  historical data. Production access starts when money does.
- If they want it finished, that is a normal quote (see quote-template.md), or the Front Desk
  productized offer if the process happens to be inbound intake.

## Scaling path (Aidan, 2026-07-26)
Once intake volume is genuinely high, build intake agents that generate the skeletons. That is what
makes this offer scale instead of becoming a treadmill, and it doubles as the best possible proof of
the product: Aevon using its own agents to run Aevon.

**Sequencing matters. Do NOT build the generator first.**
1. Hand-build the first 3-5 skeletons. Learn what actually converts: how much polish, clickable vs
   running on their data, which processes are even skeleton-able in a few days.
2. Extract the parts that repeat into reusable components.
3. Then have an agent draft from the intake, with Aidan finishing and reviewing before it ships.
   Keep the review step for a long time.

Rationale: automating before the pattern is known just produces the wrong thing faster, and an
auto-generated skeleton that feels generic destroys the exact differentiator the offer exists to
prove. Prospects can tell "he built this for us" from "this came out of a machine", and the whole
pitch rests on the first.

## How it shows up
- NOT in cold email 1 or 2 (those still lead with the built-around-your-operations argument).
- It is the REPLY offer: when someone answers with interest or a question, the drafted response
  offers to build the skeleton. That is where it converts a reply into a project.

## Cost ceiling (set 2026-08-03)
The free build must cost **nothing but time**. Aidan is willing to spend the hours; he is not
funding someone else's infrastructure to win a lead.

Hard limits on a free skeleton:
- ONE process. Not a system, not a multi-part product.
- Sample or historical data they send over. Never wired into production data.
- No paid APIs, no metered services, no licences, no hosting that bills.
- No live integration into anything that charges per call.
- Nothing that carries an ongoing running cost after it is handed over.

If what they ask for needs any of the above, say so plainly and offer to scope the paid
version instead. Agreeing to build it free anyway turns a lead offer into an unpaid project
with a bill attached, which is the opposite of the point.
