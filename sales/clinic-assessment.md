# The clinic operations assessment

**CA$1,500, fixed fee, two weeks, one clinic.**

This is not a second business. It is Tempo distribution that happens to be billable. Every
engagement produces two things: an invoice, and a qualified Tempo lead who has already walked
you through their room count, their provider mix and exactly how their schedule gets built.

If it ever starts becoming a general SMB automation retainer, stop. That version is a job with
worse pay and a boss you have to go find.

---

## Why CA$1,500 and not more

The market for fixed-fee assessments is wide and well documented. Across 157 Copilot-specific
consulting offers with published prices on Microsoft AppSource: p25 CA$3,500, median CA$7,000,
p75 CA$15,000. ProServeIT in Toronto sells a three-week Copilot Readiness Discovery at
CA$8,724. GadellNet sells vertical-specific enablement at US$1,500.

CA$1,500 sits at the floor of that range deliberately. It is a real commitment, which is the
point (paid pilots convert far better than free work), but it is small enough that a clinic
owner can approve it without a committee. Against the alternative they already understand,
Canadian SMB bookkeeping at CA$300-800/month, it reads as one month of an ordinary service
rather than a project.

Do not discount it. Do not offer it free. The commitment is most of the value.

## What they get

A written findings document, plus a one-hour walkthrough. Bounded, and the boundary is stated
up front:

1. **What you are running today.** Practice-management system, tier, what else the schedule
   actually lives in (spreadsheets, a whiteboard, a group chat, someone's memory).
2. **What you already own and have not switched on.** Concrete, per-feature, with the vendor's
   own documentation cited. Most clinics on Jane Thrive are paying for Rooms and Shift
   Management and using neither.
3. **Where the hours actually go.** Timed, not estimated: building the weekly schedule,
   resolving room clashes, chasing cover when someone calls in sick.
4. **What their software genuinely cannot do.** Jane's own docs say it cannot schedule front
   desk staff and recommend an external calendar; that it will let you double-book a treatment
   room; and that it has no room utilisation report. See [tempo-jane-gap.md](tempo-jane-gap.md).
5. **A fix list, split honestly into three:** things they can do themselves this week, things
   worth paying someone for, and things no configuration solves.

Item 5 is what makes it worth CA$1,500 rather than a sales call in a trenchcoat. If everything
in the findings conveniently points at buying Tempo, they will notice, and they should.

## How to frame it, and this part is evidence-based

A meta-analysis of message effects on psychological reactance (33 studies, 146 effect sizes,
2005-2024) found that **loss framing is not what triggers pushback**. Gain versus loss framing
showed no significant difference. What triggers pushback is language that threatens the
listener's autonomy or judgement. The same literature found that **the less credible the
critic, the stronger the reaction** — which makes an unknown solo consultant the worst possible
messenger for anything that sounds like blame.

Stanford's Zakary Tormala with Corporate Visions puts numbers on both sides. Introducing an
**unconsidered need** to a new prospect: +11% message quality, +41% perceived uniqueness, +10%
purchase intent. Attacking a decision **the buyer personally owns**: -18.8% attitude, -15.5%
likelihood to renew, +16.3% likelihood to switch away.

So the line is:

> **"These features ship switched off by default and nobody at the vendor tells you."**

Never:

> ~~"You have been paying for this for two years and not using it."~~

Same economics. Opposite outcome. The first is an unconsidered need. The second is a status
attack on the person signing your invoice.

## Scope discipline

- **One clinic, one assessment, two weeks.** Not a retainer. Not open-ended.
- **No implementation inside the fee.** Findings only. Implementation is quoted separately, and
  most of it should be Tempo.
- **No access to production patient data.** Roster, schedule and room information only. There is
  no clinical reason to touch anything else and every reason not to.
- **Cap the interview time at three hours.** The owner, the practice manager, and whoever
  actually builds the schedule each week. That last person is the one who knows.

## Who to sell it to

Only clinics. The whole justification is that each engagement doubles as Tempo qualification,
and that stops being true the moment it is sold to anyone else.

Route it through the free channels in [tempo-free-channels.md](tempo-free-channels.md), not cold
email. 371 emails into clinics across both campaigns produced zero human replies, so cold is a
dead route to this buyer regardless of what is being offered.

## What would make this worth expanding

Three paid assessments that each convert into a real Tempo conversation. If that happens, the
assessment is a working channel and deserves more hours. If three go through and none of them
turns into a Tempo discussion, then it is just consulting, and consulting is a job.
