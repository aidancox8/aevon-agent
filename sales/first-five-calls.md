# The first five calls

Prepped 2026-08-20 from `the-40.md`. Every contact name and detail below was verified on the
clinic's own site today, because roughly half the names in the-40 are scraper artifacts.

Four of the five run **Jane**. That is the pitch, and Jane's own documentation is the evidence
(`tempo-jane-gap.md`): admin and front desk staff cannot be added to the Schedule tab, rooms can
be double-booked without warning, and there is no room utilisation report.

**The goal of each call is a conversation, not a demo.** Close every one with Walling's line:
*"Who else do you know I should talk to?"*

## What to record on every call

McKenzie's question is the one that matters: **will the job buy this thing?**

1. Who gets in trouble when the schedule breaks. If nobody does, that clinic never buys.
2. How many practitioners are contractors rather than employees. Contractors bear no cost from a
   bad schedule, which is what killed the salon segment for Appointment Reminder.
3. What they already pay for without asking anyone's permission. That is the budget ceiling.
4. Whether they are on Jane Thrive, since Rooms and Shift Management are Thrive features most
   clinics pay for and never turn on.

Then quote a real price mid-conversation and stop talking. One group is shocked it costs so
much, the other says it cannot be any good that cheap. Both are data; neither is available if
the number is never said.
