# The Jane gap: Tempo's pitch, in Jane's own words

Everything below is quoted from Jane's public documentation. That is the whole point. A clinic
owner does not have to believe a stranger with no clients; they can click the link and read
their own vendor saying it.

Use this in partner conversations, association approaches, podcast appearances and the site.
Do not paraphrase the quotes. The verbatim wording plus the URL is the asset.

---

**1. Jane cannot schedule your front desk. Jane says so, and tells you to go elsewhere.**

> "Jane's scheduling features are designed around patient appointments, so there isn't a way to
> add administrative or front desk staff shifts to the Schedule tab at this time... for

`jane.app/guide/room-booking`

**3. Jane cannot tell you whether your rooms are being used.**

> "**Jane doesn't currently have a report that directly tracks room usage.**"

`jane.app/guide/room-booking`

## Why this is not a cheap shot at Jane

Jane is at 4.8/5 across 529 reviews and cannot be displaced. Nobody should try. Tempo sits
beside Jane and fills the part Jane has publicly declined to build. Say that out loud in the
pitch: it is more credible than pretending to compete, and it is what the clinic already
believes.

The mismatch worth knowing about: `jane.app/features/scheduling` markets "Staff Scheduling &
Shifts". The word doing the quiet work is **practitioner**. Prospects will have read the
marketing page, so lead with the guide pages, which settle the argument.

## It is not just Jane

The pattern is structural across every practice-management system, and it is documented in
each vendor's own help files rather than inferred:

- **OSCAR**: give a schedule to "practicing doctors, residents, nurses, **not receptionists**"
- **TELUS CHR / InputHealth**: the user types are literally "Staff, if the user does **not**
  require a schedule" versus "Practitioner, if the user **requires** a schedule"
- **Juvonno**: the Administrator profile, "best for front desk staff, managers and
  accountants", cannot have schedules
- **Cliniko**: admin staff get free unlimited logins, but no row on the calendar

The EMR data model assumes a schedulable person is a person who bills patients. A receptionist
is a set of permissions, not a resource with shifts. That is the gap, and none of ten products
closes it.

Only **Juvonno** does real room conflict detection, and it replaces the EMR entirely, so a
clinic happy on Jane will not switch to get it. That is the wedge.

## What to be careful about

- **Do not claim "no Canadian payroll sync" or "SMS is unreliable in Canada."** Zero supporting
  evidence across 1,174 scanned reviews. Both may be true; no customer is on record complaining.
- **Room pain is a wish, not a rage.** No clinic owner is publicly angry about this. It has to
  be surfaced in discovery, not assumed to arrive pre-formed.
- **Jane Payroll shipped 2026-07-31** ($40 CAD/mo + $6 per active staff, Canada excl. Quebec),
  built off Jane's timesheet data. Jane is moving into back-office. Any pitch leaning on payroll
  is weaker now, and this gap has a shelf life.
- **Price flat, never per seat.** At 30 staff a clinic gets working staff scheduling for $0
  (Sling free under 30 users, Microsoft Shifts free with Teams). Per-user pricing with part-time
  staff is the single loudest complaint across 646 Deputy reviews: "not the cheapest option if
  you have a lot of staff doing few hours, as its a per user subscription."

## How to describe the reference

**"A 75 staff multidisciplinary clinic in BC."** That is the wording. Never the clinic's name,
in any material, ever.

Do not dress it up with specifics that have not been confirmed. An earlier version of this file
said "60-provider, 11 rooms across 2 floors, 6 disciplines"; the headcount was wrong and the
rest came from a research example rather than the real deployment. Invented detail in a
reference is the one thing that cannot be walked back once a prospect checks it.

Do not claim an NDA unless one exists. "A clinic I can't name" makes the same point and is true.
Healthcare buyers deal with confidentiality constantly and read it as normal rather than evasive,
which is exactly why an anonymous reference is workable in this market and would not be in most
others. If asked directly, being straight about it ("it's where I work, so I can't use the name")
costs less than sounding cagey.

What makes it credible is not the name, it is the operational detail only an insider would know:
how a weekly schedule actually gets built, where the double-bookings come from, what happens when
someone calls in sick at 7am. Lead with that.

## The discovery question this all points at

Nobody has published a figure for hours lost to staff scheduling in a clinic. The one quantified
owner post found was about billing waste, not rostering. So the number has to come from the
75-staff deployment: hours to build a weekly schedule before and after, and double-bookings
per month eliminated. That number is the case study, and it is the one thing here that cannot be
copied from a competitor's docs.
