# Cadre: what the email says

Written 2026-08-21. Six hand-written first emails for the leads that have a verified address,
plus the rules that produced them.

## The rule that makes this campaign different

**Email one quotes their own job ad back to them.**

Every previous campaign inferred a prospect's pain from their business type: "physiotherapy
clinics usually struggle with scheduling". 3,808 sends, zero meetings. Here the opening line is
something they wrote themselves and published this month. There is nothing to guess and nothing
to be wrong about.

## Rules

- **Quote them, do not characterise them.** "You posted a role that includes tracking certification
  expiry dates" is a fact. "You're probably drowning in spreadsheets" is a guess, and they can tell.
- **Never claim a client.** Zero customers. The honest reference is "a 75 staff multidisciplinary
  clinic in BC" and nothing more: no room counts, no discipline counts, no NDA claim.
- **No price in email one.** Terms come up on a call.
- **No offer.** Same reasoning as `sales/aevon-no-offer.md`. The body states a fact, says in one
  sentence what exists, and closes with a question that is easy to answer with a no.
- **Do not mention SALUS, SiteDocs or any competitor.** Naming a competitor to a company that may
  already use one is an own goal.
- **Under 90 words.** These go to operations people mid-shift.
- **No em dashes.**

## The one thing worth saying about the product

Not "we track certificates". Against a dedicated tool that is a feature comparison this loses
(`sales/construction-safety-competitors.md`). The defensible line is that the renewal lives in the
same system as the person: onboarding, training, policies and the credential are one record, so
completing the course that renews a ticket clears the reminder without anyone re-keying it.
